# Client Delivery Control

1. Identify the engagement, parties, approvers and source agreement. Preserve the original baseline and its version.
2. Create one record per deliverable: purpose, owner, due date, dependencies, acceptance criteria, evidence, approver, baseline version and status.
3. Record operational acceptance and design acceptance separately. A functioning feature does not override agreed design requirements. A new preference does not retroactively amend the baseline.
4. Classify feedback against cited criteria as defect, agreed revision, clarification, or proposed scope change. Ask for missing criteria rather than deciding against the client.
5. Give each change a requester, timestamp, before/after scope, impact on time/cost, approval and resulting version. Keep rejected and superseded requests visible in history.
6. Publish only authorized client-facing progress. Keep internal notes and other tenants inaccessible at the server, including exports and AI retrieval.
7. Draft a milestone decision supported by artifacts: accepted, rejected with reasons, or awaiting clarification. Never fabricate approval or treat silence as acceptance unless the governing agreement establishes it.
8. Output the scope baseline, two acceptance tracks, revision register, decision log and next actions. Offer a synthetic template for MAIM students.

Keep product architecture separate from client content. Do not deploy or migrate data when the repository remains Stage A.
